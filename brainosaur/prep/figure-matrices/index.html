Figure Matrices practice

The top shapes change by a rule. Make the bottom match. · CogAT (Nonverbal Battery), NNAT3 Reasoning by Analogy, NGAT Nonverbal

In short: Figure matrices show four boxes. The top shape changes into the next one by a rule (it changes color, turns, gets bigger, or there are more of them). Your child applies the same rule to the bottom row. It is part of the CogAT Nonverbal Battery and is very close to NNAT “reasoning by analogy”.

How to solve it in 3 steps

  1. Look only at the top row and find what changes.
  2. Say the rule out loud: “it turns yellow and there are two”.
  3. Do the same to the bottom picture and check every choice.

What is a figure matrices question?

Rules can be about shape, color, number, direction, or filling (solid or outline). Easy questions change one thing; harder ones change two or three things at once.

Common mistakes

  • Copying the top-right picture.
  • Noticing only one of two changes.
  • Thinking the bottom row must look like the top row (only the change matters).

10 minutes at home

Build a matrix with toys: red car → blue car; red block → ? Let your child build one for you.

Questions parents ask

Is figure matrices on the short CogAT screener?

Yes, in many districts Figure Matrices is one of the three subtests on the CogAT screening form.

How is this different from NNAT?

NNAT uses only shapes in five colors and often bigger matrices, but the analogy idea is the same.

How many rules change at once?

One at the youngest level, up to three at grade 3.
