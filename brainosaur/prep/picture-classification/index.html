Picture Classification practice

Three pictures belong together. Which one belongs with them? · CogAT (Verbal Battery, K–2)

In short: Picture classification shows three pictures that belong to the same group. Your child picks the answer that belongs to that group too. The trick is to find the narrowest group: not just “animals” but “animals that live in water”. It is part of the CogAT Verbal Battery at kindergarten to grade 2.

How to solve it in 3 steps

  1. Name each of the three pictures out loud.
  2. Ask: what are they all? Then make the group smaller: “food” → “vegetables”.
  3. Pick the choice that fits the small group. If two seem to fit, your group is still too big.

What is a picture classification question?

The child sees three pictures in a row, for example a carrot, a pea pod and a potato. They must find what all three have in common (they are vegetables) and pick the one choice that is also in that group. Wrong choices are often close: an apple is food too, but it is a fruit, not a vegetable.

Common mistakes

  • Stopping at a big group like “food” or “animals”.
  • Choosing by color or size when the group is about what things are.
  • Not naming the pictures first.

10 minutes at home

Kitchen sort: put 3 things together (spoon, fork, knife) and ask what else belongs. Then try a smaller group: “things that keep you warm”.

Questions parents ask

What is the difference between picture classification and odd one out?

In classification the child finds the picture that belongs to a group. In odd one out (NGAT Verbal style) the child finds the one that does not belong. Both train the same skill: finding the exact group.

Which grades take the CogAT picture classification subtest?

It is used in the picture-based CogAT levels for kindergarten to grade 2. From grade 3, the verbal tests use words instead.

My child picks answers that fit “kind of”. How can I help?

Ask them to make the group smaller. Practice with three objects at home and ask “what else could go here, and why not this?”.
