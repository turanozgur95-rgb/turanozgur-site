Number Analogies practice

Groups of objects change by a rule. Use the same rule. · CogAT (Quantitative Battery, K–2)

In short: Number analogies show a 2×2 set of boxes with groups of objects. The first pair changes by a number rule (3 → 4: one more). Your child uses the same rule on the bottom row. It is part of the CogAT Quantitative Battery at kindergarten to grade 2 and is one of the three subtests in many short CogAT screeners.

How to solve it in 3 steps

  1. Count the objects in the first box and the second box.
  2. Say the rule: “3 became 5, that is 2 more.”
  3. Count the third box, use the same rule, and find the box with that many.

What is a number analogies question?

Rules children meet are: one more, one less, two more, double, half, and sharing (6 beads become 2 and 4). The objects are pictures, so the child counts rather than reads numbers.

Common mistakes

  • Guessing from how full the box looks instead of counting.
  • Mixing up “2 more” and “double” (2 → 4 could be either: check with the bottom row).
  • Copying the second box.

10 minutes at home

Snack math: show 2 crackers then 4. Ask the rule. Now show 3: how many should the second plate get?

Questions parents ask

What rules are used in CogAT number analogies?

Simple changes in quantity: adding or taking away one or two, doubling, halving and sharing a group into two parts.

Is number analogies on the CogAT screener?

Many districts give a short CogAT screening form with three subtests: Verbal Analogies or Picture Analogies, Number Analogies and Figure Matrices. Ask your school which form they use.

My child counts slowly. Is that a problem?

Accuracy first. Speed comes with practice. Teach them to count by pointing, then to see small groups at a glance.
