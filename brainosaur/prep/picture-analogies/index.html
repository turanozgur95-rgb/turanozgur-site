Picture Analogies practice

Find the picture that goes with the third one the same way. · CogAT (Verbal Battery, K–2)

In short: Picture analogies show two pictures that go together (bird → nest). Your child finds the picture that goes with a third picture in the same way (bee → hive). They appear in the CogAT Verbal Battery for kindergarten to grade 2, where children choose from 3 pictures and nothing has to be read.

How to solve it in 3 steps

  1. Look at the top pair and say a short sentence that links them: “A bird lives in a nest.”
  2. Put the bottom picture into the same sentence: “A bee lives in a …”
  3. Check every choice in the sentence. Only one should make it true. Pictures that are just about the same topic are traps.

What is a picture analogies question?

In a picture analogy the top two boxes show a pair, for example cow and milk. The bottom row shows one picture and an empty box. The child has to find the hidden link in the top pair (a cow gives us milk) and then use the very same link on the bottom picture (a hen gives us eggs). Common links are: where it lives, what it eats, what it gives us, what it grows into, who uses it, what it is part of, and a faster way to do the same job.

Common mistakes

  • Picking a picture that matches the topic (another animal) instead of the link.
  • Using a link that is too loose, like “they are both outside”. Make the sentence exact.
  • Answering before looking at all the choices.

10 minutes at home

Play “Goes with”: say a pair (dog → bone), then a new word (rabbit → ?). Take turns. Then switch the link type: “lives in”, “eats”, “used by”.

Questions parents ask

How many picture analogy questions are on the CogAT?

The number depends on the level and form. At the K–2 levels the Picture Analogies subtest has a little more than a dozen to about 18 items. Many schools use only three subtests in a short screener, and Picture Analogies is often one of them.

Does my child need to read for picture analogies?

No. At kindergarten to grade 2 the questions are pictures only, and the teacher or the tablet reads the directions. On this page you can tap Read to me to hear each question.

What is the best way to practice picture analogies?

Say the link out loud as a full sentence, then test each answer in that sentence. Short daily practice (5–10 questions) works better than long sessions.
