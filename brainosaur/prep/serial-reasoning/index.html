Serial Reasoning practice

Shapes change across a 3×3 grid. What goes in the gap? · NNAT3 Serial Reasoning, NGAT Nonverbal

In short: Serial reasoning shows a 3×3 grid of shapes that change from row to row and column to column. Your child finds the shape for the empty box. It is one of the NNAT3 question types and appears in NGAT Nonverbal matrices.

How to solve it in 3 steps

  1. Read the first full row: what changes from left to right?
  2. Read the columns too: what changes from top to bottom?
  3. Find the one choice that fits both the row and the column.

What is a serial reasoning question?

Each row and each column follows a rule: the same shapes in a new order, a shape that grows, a color that moves along. The answer must fit its row and its column.

Common mistakes

  • Checking only the row.
  • Missing that each shape appears once in every row.
  • Being fooled by a choice that copies a nearby box.

10 minutes at home

Make a 3×3 grid on paper with three toys in three colors; leave one box empty and ask which one is missing.

Questions parents ask

What is serial reasoning on the NNAT?

It is a matrix where shapes change in a sequence across rows and columns. The child finds the missing piece.

Is this harder than pattern completion?

Usually yes. It appears more at grade 2 and above.

How many choices are there?

Five, like the NNAT.
