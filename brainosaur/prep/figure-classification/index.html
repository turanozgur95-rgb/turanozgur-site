Figure Classification practice

Three shapes share a rule. Which choice follows it? · CogAT (Nonverbal Battery)

In short: Figure classification shows three shapes that share a rule, such as “all have three sides” or “all are striped”. Your child chooses the shape that follows the same rule. It is part of the CogAT Nonverbal Battery.

How to solve it in 3 steps

  1. Look at the three shapes and find what is the same in all of them.
  2. Find what is different between them: that part does not matter.
  3. Pick the choice that keeps the same thing.

What is a figure classification question?

The rule can be about the kind of shape, number of sides, color, filling, size, or direction. The other choices break the rule in one small way.

Common mistakes

  • Choosing a shape that looks like one of the three instead of following the rule.
  • Thinking color matters when it changes between the three.
  • Not checking all choices.

10 minutes at home

Sort shape cut-outs or blocks by a secret rule and let your child guess the rule.

Questions parents ask

How is figure classification different from figure matrices?

Matrices are about how a shape changes. Classification is about what three shapes have in common.

What rules appear?

Shape type, number of parts or sides, color, filling, size and direction.

Is this on NNAT?

NNAT does not have a separate classification part, but the same skill helps with its pattern questions.
