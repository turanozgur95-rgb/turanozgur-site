Cognitive Abilities Test (CogAT)

Who: K–12; picture levels for kindergarten to grade 2 (Levels 5/6–8)Usually no strict time per subtest at the young levels; the full test is given over several sessions

Three batteries: Verbal, Quantitative and Nonverbal, with three subtests each. Many districts use a short screening form with three subtests: Picture/Verbal Analogies, Number Analogies and Figure Matrices.

Questions parents ask

Which gifted test does my child's school use?

Most US districts use the CogAT, the NNAT3, the NGAT or the OLSAT, sometimes two of them. Ask the school or check the district's gifted and talented page; it usually names the test and the grade.

What is the difference between the CogAT and the NNAT?

The CogAT has three parts (verbal, quantitative and nonverbal). The NNAT3 is only nonverbal: shapes in five colors, 48 questions in about 30 minutes.

When do children take these tests?

Many districts screen all children in kindergarten, grade 1 or grade 2, often in the fall or winter. Dates are set by each district.

How should a young child practice?

Short sessions of 10–15 minutes, a few times a week, with a parent nearby. Focus on understanding each type of question, not on speed.
