Number Puzzles practice

Find the missing number that makes both sides equal. · CogAT (Quantitative Battery)

In short: Number puzzles ask for the missing number that makes both sides equal, such as 4 = 2 + ? or 1 + 3 = ? + 2. Younger children see it as two trains that must carry the same number. It is part of the CogAT Quantitative Battery.

How to solve it in 3 steps

  1. Work out the side that is complete: 1 + 3 = 4.
  2. The other side must also make 4: ? + 2 = 4.
  3. Find the number: 4 − 2 = 2. Check by putting it back.

What is a number puzzles question?

The equal sign means “the same amount on both sides”. Children who think “=” means “write the answer” often get these wrong. The puzzles grow from one-step facts to both sides having two numbers.

Common mistakes

  • Adding all the numbers you see (1 + 3 + 2).
  • Thinking “=” means “the answer comes next”.
  • Not checking the answer in the puzzle.

10 minutes at home

Use two plates as a balance: put 5 grapes on one plate and 3 on the other. How many must you add to make them equal?

Questions parents ask

What do CogAT number puzzles look like?

At the younger levels, pictures of trains or groups that must match. At grade 2 and up, short equations with a missing number on either side of the equal sign.

How can I explain the equal sign?

Say “is the same as” instead of “equals”. 1 + 3 is the same as ? + 2.

Are there symbols instead of numbers?

At higher levels some puzzles use a shape that stands for a number. The same balance idea works.
