How to teach kids to count money and make change (ages 5–10+)

Money is the most concrete kind of math a child will ever use. A child who can answer "How much is it, do I have enough, and how much change do I get?" also grows more confident with numbers in general. Here is an age-by-age roadmap.

In this guide
  1. Ages 5–6: recognising and counting
  2. Ages 7–8: paying the right amount and making change
  3. Ages 8–9: multiplication at the shop
  4. Ages 10+: decimal prices and division
  5. Practise with Little Cashier

Ages 5–6: recognising and counting

  • Name the coins and notes: which one is worth more?
  • Add up to 10: "A five and two ones make how much?"
  • Set up a pretend shop with small, round prices.

Ages 7–8: paying the right amount and making change

  • Add up a few items in a basket
  • Choose the exact amount from a wallet
  • Pay with a bigger note and work out the change

Ages 8–9: multiplication at the shop

"Three apples at $4 each" makes multiplication meaningful. Reading a real receipt together is great practice.

Ages 10+: decimal prices and division

  • Adding decimal prices ($12.50 + $7.25)
  • Division: "If 3 cost $18, how much is one?"
  • Budgeting pocket money
