NNAT3 practice for kids: the 4 shape question types, explained for parents

The NNAT3 (Naglieri Nonverbal Ability Test, Third Edition) is one of the most common gifted screeners in US school districts. It uses only shapes and colors, no words, so it can be fair to children who are still learning English. Here is exactly what it looks like and how to help your child get comfortable with it.

NNAT3 at a glance

  • 48 questions in 30 minutes, on a tablet, computer or paper
  • Every question is a picture puzzle made of geometric shapes in a few colors (typically black, white, yellow, blue and green)
  • 5 answer choices for each question
  • Levels by grade: Level A (kindergarten), B (1st grade), C (2nd grade), D (3rd–4th grade)

The four NNAT3 question types

  1. Pattern Completion: a piece has been cut out of a large design. Which piece fills the hole? (Most common at Levels A and B.)
  2. Reasoning by Analogy: a 2×2 matrix where a shape changes across a row; apply the same change down the column.
  3. Serial Reasoning: a 3×3 matrix where shapes change step by step along rows and columns; find the missing cell.
  4. Spatial Visualization: shapes are combined, overlapped or turned; which answer shows the result? (More common at higher levels.)

How to practise NNAT question types at home

  1. Teach a checklist: shape, color, size, number, direction. Check one feature at a time.
  2. Look across, then down: in matrix questions, find the rule in the rows, then check it in the columns.
  3. Get used to 30 minutes: 48 questions in 30 minutes is about 37 seconds per question. A few full-length timed practice runs in the last weeks help a lot.
  4. Guess and move on: there is no penalty for a wrong guess on most screeners; getting stuck costs time.
  5. Keep it short and playful: 15–20 minutes a day the rest of the time.

Frequently asked questions

Does the NNAT need reading or English?

No. The test uses only shapes and colors; only the short directions are given by the teacher or the screen.

Which NNAT level is my child taking?

Usually Level A in kindergarten, B in 1st grade, C in 2nd grade and D in 3rd–4th grade. Your district can confirm.

Is the NNAT the same as the NGAT?

No. Both were developed by Dr. Jack Naglieri, but the NGAT is a newer, separate set of verbal, quantitative and nonverbal tests.
